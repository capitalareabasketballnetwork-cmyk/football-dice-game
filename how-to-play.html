OFFICIAL EARLY ACCESS GUIDE

HOW TO PLAY.

DieBall is football reduced to its essentials: choose your play, roll dice, use penalty cards strategically, and move the ball down the field. Here's the complete guide to the game's current rules.

3 vs. 2Three offensive dice versus two defensive dice
2 PassesMaximum per offensive possession
4 DownsGet ten yards or give up possession

First game walkthrough

1
Pick a game type

Choose You vs. CPU to face DieBall Don, or 1v1 Local to share one device. For local play, enter home and away team names first.

2
Check possession and field position

The top scoreboard identifies the teams. The colored down-and-distance box tells you how far the offense must advance for a new first down.

3
Choose run or pass

Run is your default. A pass can be more rewarding, but a failed pass loses five yards and you have only two per drive.

4
Roll the dice

On offense the three dice total is compared with the two defensive dice. The field arrow tracks the last gain or loss. On local phone play, both players press Roll Dice to trigger one simultaneous result.

5
Use penalty cards once between dice plays

Choose any number of remaining penalty cards; the opponent can counter. Compare the totals to determine movement. Cards can earn a first down but never directly cause a touchdown or safety.

6
Finish the drive

Score, continue earning first downs, punt, try a field goal, or turn the ball over on fourth down. You can also lose possession on a fumble.

Examples: seeing the game in action

RUN EXAMPLE

A six-yard run

You roll 5, 4, and 3 for an offensive total of 12. Don rolls 4 and 2 for a defensive total of 6. The result is +6 yards. From your own 35, you move to your own 41.

5 + 4 + 3 = 12
4 + 2 = 6
12 − 6 = +6 yards
PASS EXAMPLE

Completing a big pass

Offense rolls a total of 14, defense rolls 8. Since the difference is at least four, the pass gains twice the difference: 12 yards. If the difference were only three, you would lose five yards instead.

(14 − 8) × 2 = +12 yards
Net under 4 → −5 yards
FIRST DOWN EXAMPLE

Using penalty cards

You have 2nd & 7. You play an 8 and a 4 while Don commits a 5. The net is +7, reaching the line to gain without using another down: now it's 1st & 10 (unless near the goal line).

(8 + 4) − 5 = +7 yards
2nd & 7 → 1st & 10
FUMBLE EXAMPLE

A recovery die of five

The offense's three dice total 9 and the defense's two dice also total 9. A fumble is announced. Press Roll Die; if the recovery die lands on 5, the other team gets the ball. If it lands on 2, the offense keeps the ball but loses the down.

Raw totals tie: 9 = 9
Recovery 5 → Ball Lost
Recovery 2 → Ball Retained

Dice and yardage

RUN

Standard rushing play

Roll 3 offensive dice and 2 defensive dice. Subtract the defense's sum from the offense's sum. The result can be positive or negative.

Offense 13 − Defense 8 = +5 yards
PASS

High risk, higher reward

Choose Pass before rolling. If the adjusted offensive total minus the defense total is at least 4, gain twice the difference. Otherwise lose 5 yards. No more than two passes per possession.

Net 6 → +12 yards · Net 3 → −5 yards
BONUS

Triple matching dice

If all three offensive dice match, the game announces “Triple die!” and shows a light-green ×2 beside the offense total. Double the offensive sum before subtracting the defense. Defensive matching dice receive no bonus; a raw tie still causes a fumble.

3 + 3 + 3 = 9 → adjusted offense 18
SPECIAL

Fumbles

If the original raw totals match, the play becomes a fumble before any triple bonus. For a failed pass, the offense first loses 5 yards, even on a fumble; recovery then happens at that new spot. A loss of five yards into your own end zone is a safety. Press Roll Die for recovery: 1–4 retains possession but loses a down, while 5–6 loses the ball at the new spot.

Raw offense 10 = raw defense 10 → Fumble!
On a successful pass that travels beyond the back boundary of the opponent's end zone, the pass is incomplete. It still costs a down.

First downs and cards

First downs

You have four downs to advance ten yards. Getting enough yards resets the count to 1st down and creates a new target. Failing to reach it after fourth down causes a turnover on downs.

Penalty cards

Each team receives three private cards at the start of a drive, from a shared shuffled deck. A card has a value from 2 to 11. Players can commit one, two, or three cards together, but only once between dice plays.

Card battles

The offensive card total minus the defensive card total determines yards awarded or taken away. Cards do not consume a down, can grant first downs, and stop at either 1-yard line instead of scoring directly.

Keeping your hand secret

In 1v1 Local phone mode, each side has a separate hand with an eye button to hide and reveal cards. Put the cards away before the other player looks. Remaining cards are discarded when a drive ends.

Scoring

22-POINT CONVERSION

After the touchdown announcement finishes, choose between a timing-based extra-point kick or one offensive play for two points (from the opponent's 5). Both teams receive different private hands and may commit remaining penalty cards before the conversion. If you still have passes left, you may choose Run or Pass on the two-point try; a successful pass needs a net of at least +4, gains double yardage, and displays a light-green ×2 beside the offensive dice total. The conversion dice are rolled only after the cards are committed and the player presses Roll Dice. In Local and TV modes, both teams press their Roll Dice controls to ready the extra-point attempt before the offense uses the kicking meter. A fumble during the try fails it.

Kicking and changing possession

Field goals

Available on any down while on offense. You can cancel before taking the kick. Stop the moving timing marker inside the green window. The ball visibly travels between two small yellow dots representing the uprights from above or swerves away on a miss. Longer attempts have smaller success zones. A miss gives the opponent the ball at the previous spot.

Punts

Punts now use a fast vertical timing meter, like kickoffs. Stop the moving marker to select a punt of approximately 10–95 yards; the red top segment represents out of bounds and the colored labels line up with each distance zone. You can cancel before committing the punt. The ball flies into the end zone or beyond the sideline before resetting to the correct next spot. A punt beyond the end zone results in a touchback to the receiving team's own 35. There are no returns or bounces.

Kickoffs

Stop the fast vertical marker to choose where the other team receives the ball. Aim close to their own 1-yard line. The meter includes out-of-bounds (midfield) and end-zone (own 35) zones and color-coded landing ranges. The ball travels out of bounds or into the end zone before resetting to its awarded yard line.

Safeties

If an offensive dice play forces the ball into the offense's own end zone, the defense earns 2 points. The conceding team then kicks off from its own 20.

Game format and modes

DieBall games are organized into four quarters with scheduled possessions. The current web beta starts with two offensive possessions per team per quarter. More game-length and online-play options are planned.

  • You vs. CPU: play against DieBall Don, who can run, pass, use cards, punt, or attempt field goals.
  • 1v1 Local: play on one device with home and away names. On the phone screen, both players have their own dice and hidden cards. Both press Roll Dice before results resolve together.
  • DieBall TV (beta): one device hosts a room and shows the board; two players join by code from their own phones. The host stays open and the phones control each team. Rooms are temporary and use DieBall's shared same-origin room relay, which needs internet access. Room data expires and is not added to historical account statistics.
  • 1v1 Online: requires real account authentication and online matchmaking; not yet active.
  • Season: future mode for schedules and progression; not yet active.
